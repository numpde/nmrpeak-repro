Bounded provider recovery
=========================

ObservationRecovery.gap_seconds bounds the entire fresh-observation operation,
including HTTP exchange, retirement and retry pacing. ConditionRecovery uses
window_seconds for retry dispatch and pacing after the first failed condition
publication. It does not bound the initial publication, a final in-flight
exchange, or its retirement; those retain their transport-owned bounds. NMRPeak previously
passed observation_retry_initial_seconds as the observation gap and condition-retry
window; its name hid that meaning. The SDK already owns retry admission, backoff and exact Attempt
identity. NMRPeak does not add another API retry loop.

The operator and captured runtime now require observation_gap_seconds. The
backoff cap remains observation_retry_max_seconds and is independent of the
gap. Old ambiguous fields are rejected. Update the host-owned provider template
and capture new inputs through the normal deployment workflow before selecting
the new image. Do not edit retained immutable runtime trees. Existing running
providers retain their captured policy until redeployed.

The example gap is 90 seconds: with its 30-second exchange and 10-second
retirement budgets this leaves time for two bounded exchanges and normal retry
pacing. Server Retry-After may consume the remainder instead. This is a chosen
example policy, not a universal SDK minimum; increasing the gap also increases
possible cancellation-detection latency. Choose deployed budgets against the
actual admitted transport settings.

Delivery acknowledgement releases only shared report backpressure. It does
not prove that the original controller, scientific worker, or interpreter
has retired, or that the API has applied the report. The controller and its
claim remain owned until their own settlement rules are satisfied.

Continuing a failed lane after acknowledgement remains unresolved when its
ControllerFailed also retains an independent or cleanup failure. A later
cleanup observation must distinguish a historical diagnostic from an owner
whose retirement is still unconfirmed. An acknowledgement alone cannot make
that decision; preserve the failure and original owners until a concrete
classification and recovery witness establish when continuation is safe.
